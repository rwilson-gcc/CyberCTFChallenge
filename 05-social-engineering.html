Social Engineering

Learn how attackers exploit human psychology — trust, authority, urgency — to bypass technical security entirely.

The Human Firewall

Technical defences can be perfect, but a single employee tricked into revealing credentials can bring an entire organisation down. Social engineering targets human psychology, not software. Over 90% of successful cyberattacks involve social engineering at some stage.

🎭 Pretexting

Creating a fabricated scenario to extract information — "I'm from IT, I need your login to fix your account."

🎁 Baiting

Leaving infected USB drives in car parks or offering "free software" that contains malware.

🚪 Tailgating

Physically following an authorised person through a secure door — exploiting politeness.

📞 Vishing

Voice phishing — impersonating bank staff, HMRC, or tech support to extract data over the phone.

Psychological Triggers Used

⚡ Urgency

"Your account will be deleted in 1 hour!" — rushing people past their critical thinking.

🎖 Authority

Impersonating IT, management, or government agencies to make requests seem legitimate.

😰 Fear

"We've detected fraud on your account" — fear overrides rational decision-making.

🤝 Reciprocity

Doing a small favour first so the victim feels obligated to help in return.
